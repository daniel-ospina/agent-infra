## Phase 3.5: Competitor Intelligence Mining

### 3.5.1 Identify top 5-8 competitors most relevant to current JTBD (read existing competitor profiles)

### 3.5.2 Research: What They Understand (heavy, 25-40 queries)
- **Before any web research:** check for existing competitor profile files in the codebase (e.g., a `competitive/` directory with per-competitor profiles and an index/analysis). Read the relevant profiles for each competitor identified in 3.5.1. Extract facts, positioning, features, pricing, and target segments already documented — do not re-research these.
- Web searches should focus on the **JTBD/worldview lens** — what competitors UNDERSTAND about the market, their blind spots, how their PoV differs from ours — not on re-gathering facts already captured in profiles.
- 3-5 queries per competitor: observable evidence of what they understand (target segments in marketing, language in sales pages, case study metrics, pricing model implications)
- 5-8 queries: perspectives on the target market NOT represented by any major player
- 3-5 queries: how might our PoV (from philosophy.md) represent a different market understanding?
- 3-5 queries: adjacent markets or analogous businesses that solved similar problems

**Important:** Competitor insights from desk research are `inferred`, not `earned secrets`. Mark them as `[desk-research]`. Reserve earned secrets for field-validated insights.

### 3.5.3 Adversarial Review: Worldview Battle
- Agent 1: steelmans competitors' worldview. **Must include:** "List 3 things competitors might know about this market that we don't, based on their longer operating history and larger customer base."
- Agent 2: defends our differentiated PoV
- Agent 3: synthesizes — where genuinely different? Where telling ourselves a story?

### 3.5.4 Output → feeds §4, §5, research dump. Desk-research insights tagged appropriately.
